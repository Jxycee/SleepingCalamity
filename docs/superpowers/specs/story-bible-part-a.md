# Story bible, Part A: tone, the deal, the heroes, the enemy, Hollowmere and the Undercroft

**Status: draft for the user (2026-11-03).** It builds on `docs/STORY.md` (locked) and the dungeon spec (`2026-11-03-hearthdeep-dungeon-design.md`). Every block is tagged *settled* (already in STORY or the spec, restated) or *proposal* (new here). Nothing here changes STORY until the user accepts it; anything that would bend locked text is listed in section 8. Part B (towns 2-6) writes against the pins in section 9.

The brief (user, 2026-11-03): an adult-grade story with morally grey people, consequences that stick and violence that costs, told through a cast the player grows fond of, a clear quest and one small story per town. The tone target is a reference for maturity only: no names, plots, scenes or prose borrowed from it or from Dark Cloud. Rating: mature teen to adult. Violence and dread, yes. No sexual content (relationships stop at longing, a kiss, a betrayal), no lingering gore on screen; cruelty shows in the choices people make.

## 1. Tone

### 1.1 What changes from STORY section 1 (*proposal*, amendment candidate A1)
STORY 1 says: whimsical on the surface, dark undertow, whimsy as coping. Keep all of that for the **art and the play**: sunlit squares, saturated day colour, storybook shapes, warm music, bread that smells good. Change one thing in the **writing**: the darkness moves from the undertow to the centre. The grief and the guilt are what the story is about, not a shadow under it.

- **Nobody is clean.** Every adult who comes home did something to keep the deal running: they watered grain, hid a loft of barley, kept quiet, looked away. The four heroes are not clean either: each has done or will do something that costs another person.
- **Consequences stick.** A resident can be taken back. A plot can stay empty for the rest of the game. One name on Wren's list will be painted out. Choices made in anger are not undone by the next scene.
- **Violence costs.** Fights against nightmares stay fun and crunchy. Violence between people is rare, quick and ugly in its results (a man who never walks right again, a town that can't look at itself), never cool.
- **The contrast is the point.** The worst things are said in the nicest places: a confession over warm bread in a sunny square. The art never darkens to match the writing.

Why: the user asked for adult-grade people with an engaging quest. The settled premise (a town that sold its harvest, then its people) already is that story. STORY 1 kept it at arm's length.

### 1.2 Voice rules (*proposal*)
1. **Townsfolk talk plain and short.** Trades, weather, food, neighbours. They never make speeches about their morals. They lie by leaving things out, not by inventing.
2. **Wishes stay plain** (settled, STORY 8.3): "I want the oven facing east." The story lives in what they say after the wish is met.
3. **Humour comes from people under strain:** gallows jokes, bickering between people who love each other, insults aimed at the powerful or at yourself. Pip jokes to avoid; Tobin jokes when he's too tired to argue; Ash's jokes are dry and usually true; Wren almost never jokes, so when she does it lands.
4. **Never played for laughs:** the taken children, Nell Marsh, the tally, hunger and famine, a real death, the feast food, Isolde's choice, Corwin Vane's sixty years awake.
5. **Swearing** is allowed sparingly when people are hurt or terrified. Everyday oaths are Vale-made ("rot it", "gods' teeth", "by the bell").
6. **Mawrrow never shouts.** It is polite, warm and hospitable, like a host who has already eaten (section 4.1).
7. **No villain explains himself fully.** Vane and the Gleaners give reasons that are true and incomplete.
8. **No modern slang, no winks at the player.**

## 2. World history and the deal

### 2.1 The Vale (*settled*)
A ring of valleys around an old mountain, towns linked by one road; thin soil, short summers, a hungry winter every few years (STORY 2).

**Calendar (*proposal*):** dates in this bible are **pact years (PY)**. The Bargain is PY 0 (about sixty years before the game), the Long Night is PY 50, and the game opens in PY 60.

### 2.2 The Bargain: the terms everyone knew (*settled*, STORY 2)
Rich fields for a yearly tithe carried into the deep; feast-eaters counted as party; the tithe grew from a tenth to a half; the towns cheated and the elders kept quiet.

### 2.3 The terms nobody read (*proposal*)
The Bargain was written in Mawrrow's own script (2.4), and only Corwin Vane could read it when he signed. The clause that matters:

> If the tithe falls short, the table is forfeit.

The **table** is everyone who ate at that year's feast. The grain was never the price. The grain was the interest; the people at the table were the collateral. The Tithe Feast was not a thanksgiving. It was each town signing for its own body, once a year, with the first bite.

**The binding rule (*proposal*, clarification C1).** A person is bound from the feast they eat at until the next feast. At each feast the old binding ends and the new one begins with the meal. The tithe is weighed after the meal. So a person who skips one feast's meal is bound to nothing that year, even if they ate at every feast before. This is why four children who had eaten at earlier feasts could be "uncounted" on the Long Night (STORY 2: "they never ate, so they were never counted").

**The tally.** Before each feast the town's ledger-keeper wrote every resident's name in the script, in plain ink, and the page went down with the tithe carts. At the first bite, the demon's ember burned over each eater's name and sealed it. In the dream that page is the tally on floor 8: every name in Hollowmere burning, and four names still in faded plain ink, blank against the rest (STORY 8.4's "four left blank").

### 2.4 The script (*proposal*)
The script is the demon's handwriting: the letters of the contract. Vane learned to read it the night he signed. Isolde Hale, Hollowmere's ledger-keeper in the last years, taught herself to read it from fifty years of tally pages, and found she could also write it. Written by a human hand, the demon's letters do small, real things: a charm against wild animals, a ward on a door, a light that won't go out. This is the script on Tuppence's collar and on the underside of the church trapdoor (STORY 8.5), and it is the root of Wren's magic (3.8). The heroes' best weapon against Mawrrow is its own alphabet, and that should make everyone uneasy.

### 2.7 The Long Night (*settled* frame, *proposal* detail)
PY 50, the Tithe Feast. *Settled:* the tithe fell short, Mawrrow took Hollowmere in one night, then fell into its glutted sleep; the other towns fell over the following weeks, each to its own debt (STORY 2). *Proposal:* Hollowmere's shortfall was found at the weighing after the meal, under the lanterns, with the long tables still full. The bell was ringing the end of the feast when every lantern in the Vale's first town went out at once.

### 2.8 Why the four were uncounted (*proposal*; the gutting answer)
*Settled:* the four slipped away before the meal, Pip had heard spooky tales of the founders' tunnels and talked the others in, Wren's lantern stayed lit, and they never ate (STORY 2, 8.5). Floor 8 shows the four blank names.

*Proposal:* **it was not chance.** At the harvest weighing Isolde Hale saw the tithe would fall short, and voted with the council to water the grain anyway, knowing it might not pass. That night she told three people she trusted, and between them they got their own children away from the table:
- **Odile Ostrander**, Pip's grandmother, the watch sergeant who counted heads at the feast gate, told Pip the tunnel story the week before, knowing Pip would drag the others along. She packed her old watch dagger and shield into Pip's bag "for the spooky things".
- **Hob Marsh**, Tobin's father and Isolde's secret partner of two years, gave Tobin his best axe "for the roots down there" and told him to **take his little sister Nell**.
- **Jory Calder**, Ash's older brother and Isolde's surveying apprentice, walked the tunnels' safe stretch the day before and gave Ash his hunting kit, "since you're always stealing it".
- **Isolde** gave Wren her surveyor's staff with the lantern hung on its hook.

The plan was **five** children. Tobin, ten years old and dragging an axe, left five-year-old Nell crying by the trestles because she was slow. Nell ate. Nell was taken.

Why only their own: four or five children wandering off is mischief. Ten is a missing table that the elders, the watch and the deep would notice before the meal. Adults were counted at the gate, and Isolde wrote the tally herself: a missing adult was a missing signature. So the four adults sat down, ate, and were taken, and every other child in Hollowmere ate beside them. **The heroes are alive because their parents chose them over everyone else's children**, including the heroes' friends, whose names are on Wren's wall.

The grey edge: Isolde could read the whole contract. It may also say that an unbound person standing at the table can speak against it (STORY 6 ch5: "whoever stands before Mawrrow awake, unbound, can end it"). Wren will never know whether her mother saved her as a daughter or made her as a weapon. Isolde, asked in ch4, gives an answer that could be either.

**How it lands across the game (*proposal*):** ch1 shows only the blanks (settled truth: contract, uncounted), plus seeds the player can't yet read: five small ticks in Isolde's hand beside the four blanks, one crossed out (floor 8); the strange gifts of a real dagger and a real axe to small children; Hob's line after the boss (6.10). The heirloom memories carry it (3.6). The full truth lands in ch4 (Isolde) and is said aloud by all four in ch5.

### 2.9 Why the heirlooms survived, and why three owners are elsewhere (*proposal*, amendment candidate A4)
The heirlooms survived because they were in the tunnels with the children, given to them by the conspirators (2.8). That is also the first clue: who hands a seven-year-old a real blade?

Three owners (Odile, Jory, Isolde) come home in chapters 2, 3 and 4, so they can't be in the Undercroft. Reason: the steward sorted the taken. The four adults who emptied seats were cheats, and he laid three of them out along the road as **bait**: Odile in the Drowned Arcade, Jory in the Glow Seams, Isolde in the Hive Choir, so the children would have to come all the way to him. Hob he kept at home as the knot in the Tithe Roots (floor 9). In ch1 the heroes only notice the gap: Wren's list and the Undercroft's "people left" counter disagree by three names.

### 2.10 Why the church stands (*proposal*, amendment candidate A3)
STORY 8.2 says every building was wiped, yet the church and its tower stand. Reason: the week before the feast Isolde warded the church cellar in the script to keep the deep from rising through the old mine-mouth. The ward held the building; it saved nobody inside the pact. The bell, hung in the open tower above the wards, was taken. The storm in the opening (STORY 8.5) is Mawrrow's turn in its sleep breaking those wards, which is why the trapdoor stands open.

**The lit lanterns in the church** (*proposal*): the deep lit them. In the Vale a feast begins when the lanterns are lit. It is an invitation, and there are four places at it.

### 2.11 Wren's lantern (*proposal*, amendment candidate A2)
Keep it a lighter mystery. Wren wasn't using magic (settled). In ch4, asked straight, Isolde says: "I filled the oil. That's all I did." She may be lying. The player never learns more. The only other hint: in the finale, when the heirlooms strike (STORY 7.3), Wren's staff burns the same warm colour as that lantern. Easter egg, not answer.

## 3. The heroes

Each hero's age, role, heirloom and want are *settled* (STORY 3); everything below is *proposal*.

### 3.1 Wren Hale (18)
- **Wound:** her mother chose her, and Wren has spent ten years trying to deserve it without knowing it. The list is penance as much as hope.
- **Secret:** the script she casts with is the demon's own (2.4). She worked that out years ago and never told the others.
- **What she has done:** woke Tuppence. Ten years of quiet broke because Wren wanted her cat back (STORY 8.5: the first stir, the storm). She knows it. She will do it again for every person on the wall.
- **What she will do (ch4):** she finds the ledger page with the five ticks and Isolde's notes, understands the conspiracy, and **hides it** from the other three for a whole chapter, to protect her mother and herself. Pip finds the page in ch5.
- **Flaw:** control. She plans other people's lives and calls it care.
- **Change:** from "exactly as they were" to "home as they are". She paints out one name in ch5 (Abner Stroud, 4.4) with her own brush.

### 3.2 Tobin Marsh (20)
- **Wound:** his mother Jessamy died of fever in PY 47. At seven he was already half-raising Nell; at ten he was raising three other children.
- **Secret:** he left Nell by the trestles (2.8). He has never told anyone. Her name is on Wren's wall and he walks past it every night.
- **Flaw:** martyrdom with a temper. He takes every load so nobody can see him drop one, then hates them for letting him.
- **What he will do (ch1-2):** lie to Nell when she comes home and asks why he left; let his father believe Nell just wandered off; hit Ash in ch3 (3.7).
- **Change:** ch1 he wants Hob to take the weight back and learns Hob can't (a hard, frightened man ten years out of time, drinking again by the second week). He confesses Nell to Wren alone. Ch5 he tells all four. He stays the grown-up, but by choice and with help.

### 3.3 Pip Ostrander (17)
- **Wound:** her mother left the Vale for downriver when Pip was two. Gran raised her. Finding the tunnels is the one thing Pip did that mattered.
- **Secret:** she knows Ash hears the demon (3.4) and has kept it for him for three years.
- **The cut:** in ch2 she learns Gran planted the tunnel story. She isn't the reason they're alive; Gran is, and Gran made her the bait for the others.
- **What she will do (ch2):** in Saltwake she unmasks the harbour reeve who kept the second ledger, in front of the waking town, because it feels good. They beat him in the street. He lives, lame, and is driven out down the river road; his plot stays empty for the rest of the game (Part B to set the details). Pip has to walk past it.
- **Flaw:** she says the true thing at the worst moment, and she enjoys it.
- **Change:** from proving herself by being loudest to the one who keeps her mouth shut when a secret is someone else's to tell (she gives Wren a night in ch5 before she shows the page).

### 3.4 Ash Calder (19)
- **Wound:** Jory gave him the kit and walked him to the tunnel and Ash, nine, thought it was a gift. It was a goodbye.
- **Secret 1:** for three years Ash has had a place in the high scree (a warm fissure) where the dreaming demon's voice comes up through the rock. He listens, and sometimes he answers. It has told him true things.
- **Secret 2, unknown to him:** his mother Hester was Corwin Vane's granddaughter. The man Ash is hunting is his great-grandfather (lands ch4, with Vane).
- **What he will do (ch3):** he trades with the voice for the place where Jory sleeps, and pays with a name off Wren's wall. The lamp-keeper Wynn Cobb, already home, is **taken back** into the deep one night: his house goes dark, his service (collapse seconds) is lost, and he isn't found again until the Hearthdeep (ch6). This is a one-time scripted story event, never caused by any dive outcome; "safe on pickup" still holds for every dive (amendment candidate A11). Tobin breaks Ash's nose for it.
- **Flaw:** curiosity that treats people as answers.
- **Change:** from "who made the Bargain" to "what do I owe for it". In ch5 he faces Vane as family, and has to decide whether Vane's sixty years count for anything.

## 4. The demon and its servants

Section 4's frames are *settled* in STORY 4; the detail is *proposal*.

### 4.1 Mawrrow, the Hungering
- **Nature:** Mawrrow can only eat what is owed. It cannot take what isn't given, so it makes people give. Contracts are its teeth. It does not hate the Vale; it finds it delicious, and it is patient.
- **Voice:** a host's. Warm, unhurried, full of table manners. It invites, it asks if you're comfortable, it compliments your strength the way a cook compliments a joint. It never threatens directly. In dreams it speaks to each hero about the thing they want most and offers a fair-sounding price.
- **Why the deal was a trap:** the tithe was set to grow with every feeding, and its hunger grows faster than any harvest can. Default was certain from PY 0. The forfeit clause made the people the real meal; fifty years of rich harvests were fattening.
- **What it wants:** the whole Vale at its table, every seat filled and the contract closed, and then the lands beyond the mountain, because a full belly makes it strong enough to rise and look for the next valley.
- **Why it wants the four:** Hollowmere's table is four seats short. An open debt is a flaw in its law, and an unbound person standing at its table can speak against the contract. The four are both its unpaid debt and the only weapon in the Vale.

### 4.2 Elder Corwin Vane, the Tithe-Keeper
- **Then:** in the famine of PY -1 he buried his wife and his youngest son and watched his daughter go thin. When the voice under the mountain answered the harvest prayer, he was the only one who could read its letters, and he read the forfeit clause before he signed. He signed anyway. He told himself the Vale would grow strong enough to pay. He also liked being the man who saved it.
- **The cover-up:** he wrote the elder's oath that passed the clause down in secret, and he named the Tithe Feast a thanksgiving.
- **What the Vale believes:** the Bargain was the six elders' joint act. Vane is remembered as the elder who walked the first tithe down in PY 1 "to see it settled" and never came back up; a memorial stone stands in Hollowmere's churchyard and Bram Tolley's pageant made him a martyr. His name can appear in ch1-3 as that historical figure. Ch4 reveals he is alive, the steward, and the maker of the deal (STORY 6).
- **Now:** a hundred and four years old in a body that stopped at forty-four, with no heart and no sleep. During the glutted sleep he stood still in the dark under Stillhollow like a stopped clock, eyes shut but awake. The bell at the end of ch1 opens them (STORY 8.4: the man who should be dead; *settled* beat, unnamed in ch1).
- **What he is owed:** Mawrrow promised to give back his heart, and let him die, when the last table is closed. His motive is not power. It is rest, and it is the hope that his family, his great-grandchildren Ash and Jory among them, will sleep sweetly in the dream rather than in pain.
- **What he loses (ch5):** the steward's hold, his reason, and his family's regard: Ash looks at him and doesn't call him anything. Whether he is allowed to die at last is Part B's choice.

### 4.3 The Gleaners
- **Who they are:** taken people who were awake enough inside the dream to be offered a **second bargain**: serve, and the ones you love sleep sweet and come home first. Every Gleaner chose it for someone. That's the ch2 truth.
- **How they hunt:** they don't try to kill the four; they try to **feed** them. One bite of the demon's food seats a hero at the table. Their grab attack is a force-feeding: a grab with a struggle-out, never a gore beat. **Game rule:** a failed struggle-out is an ordinary down (revivable, counted like any other). It never binds a hero to the pact and never removes a hero; the threat of being seated is story, not a mechanic.
- **Look:** demon-made harvest workers (horns, scorched hide, ember cracks, sickles and flails) wearing wheat-sheaf masks. Under the mask the face is still human (amendment candidate A6). The common ones are faceless masks; the lieutenants unmask.

### 4.5 The nightmares
The red breath is Mawrrow's digestion. The dream chews a town's things and they come out as guards: the idea stays (a jam jar, a broom, an ox), the make is demon. Lore line for the journal: "It doesn't make monsters. It makes leftovers."

## 6. The Undercroft, floor by floor

The floor plan and dive rules are *settled* (STORY 8.4, spec 4-5): safe on pickup, the bank after floor 5 as the only exit, per-player loot, shared keys/pieces/people, stir. Nothing here changes them. Generated floors (2, 4, 6, 7) tell their story through **recurring props and room kits**, never one fixed scene. Anything not in STORY or the spec is *proposal*.

### 6.1 Floor 1: the cellar under the church (authored teaching floor)
- **Beat:** the four go down into the house of their childhood's ghosts. The cellar is the town's root cellar, folded and wet.
- **Sees and hears:** the sluice key; a cellar that floods and drains; the key carrier; red glow through a drain grate (settled). A sealed alcove with a sigil (settled: a later dungeon's mark). Dripping, a far-off slow heartbeat that is the demon breathing.
- **Environment:** feast lanterns hung on cellar hooks, still dripping wax. Chalk marks on beams: a carter's tally of barrels.
- **Moment:** at the drain grate, Pip: "Well. It's a portal to hell. Called it."

### 6.2 Floor 2: larders and cold rooms (generated)
- **Kit:** hams on hooks, cheese wheels with bite marks too big for rats, salt barrels, crocks of jam. **Recurring prop:** chalk-dusted flour sacks stamped with Hollis's mark (his chalked tithe loaves; pays off when he wakes).
- **Enemies:** pantry nightmares (settled), most human-scale things here: jam-jar slimes, rat-kings, the sweeping broom.
- **Beat:** the first weapon level-up (spec 5 hook). The survivors' bread barely heals, so the player learns to dodge.

### 6.3 Floor 3: the drowned bakery (set piece; rescue: Hollis Rye)
- **Beat:** route the water to reach the sleeping baker (settled). He is wrapped in risen dough gone to stone, mid-knead.
- **Sees:** cold ovens underwater, loaves floating like drowned birds, the bakery's sign upside down.
- **Moment:** Hollis wakes, looks at Tobin, and says the first adult thing anyone has said to them in ten years: "You've got big. Who's been feeding you? It's not been done right." Then he sees the empty square through the trapdoor light later and sits down on the floor.
- **Hook:** from here on he's safe (settled); a wipe on floor 5 doesn't lose him.

### 6.4 Floor 4: wine vaults and upper cisterns (generated; the water starts bubbling)
- **Kit:** racks of casks; **recurring prop:** false casks that open on hidden grain sacks (the council's hidden store, 2.6). Steam vents, warm pools that hurt (settled).
- **Enemies:** pantry nightmares, drip-spirits, eels in the dark water (settled).
- **Beat:** carrying loot toward a guarded bank with no exit yet (spec 5 hook): tension, not a decision. Ash, finding a false cask: "Look at that. Even the cellar was lying."

### 6.5 Floor 5: the cistern dome, the Brazen Ox, the innkeeper, the bank
- **Set piece (settled):** the tithe-cart oxen remade as one hollow brass demon with a fire in its belly, boiling the dome. Flood its fire through the sluices; the quench cracks its hide. No retry (settled).
- **Story meaning:** the oxen pulled the tithe down the ramp every autumn for fifty years. The dream remade the beasts that carried the town's food to the demon into the thing that cooks it.
- **Environment:** tithe-cart wheels as dome ribs; yoke chains forged by Dun Harrow (stamped with his mark; the player sees it again on his anvil).
- **Rescue (settled):** Marta Fenn, sleeping at a long table in the dome's side vault, ladle still in hand. Waking, she counts the four of them twice and then asks, "Where's the little one? Hob's girl." Tobin says he doesn't know. (His first spoken lie on screen.)
- **The bank (settled):** full heal, bank slots, then go home or push on; pushing on saves and locks the bank.

### 6.6 Floors 6-7: coal cellars and flues (generated, heat)
- **Kit:** coal chutes, bread ovens big enough to walk into, flues, dampers, bellows (settled tool). **Recurring props:** scorched feast banners, roasting spits, and on 7, small chairs (a children's table) set before an oven that never opens. No children shown; only the chairs.
- **New heat creatures (*proposal*, to design):** *spitjacks* (turnspits that run on legs of skewers), *scuttlers* (coal scuttles with ember mouths), *kettle-imps* (kettles that scream steam), *flue-wraiths* (soot shapes that crawl the chimneys).
- **People:** Aldous, Bram, Wynn and Nell come home from these depths (5.2). Nell's pickup is the floor's quiet stop: a small girl asleep in a coal bin, and Tobin's hands shake.

### 6.7 Floor 8: the feast hall (set piece; story, no rescue)
- **Beat:** work dampers and flames to reach the tally (settled). The sunk feast is laid out under braziers that never go out: long tables, every place set, food steaming after ten years.
- **The food:** it is never edible and the game never lets a hero eat it. Pip reaches for a pear out of habit; Wren slaps her hand away without knowing why. (She'll know in ch2.)
- **The tally (settled):** every name in Hollowmere, with four left blank. Wren reads it aloud in the script. The truth for ch1 lands here: the feast was a contract, and the four were never counted.
- **How it looks (*proposal*, 2.3):** every name burns with the demon's ember seal; four stand in faded plain ink. Ash runs a finger over his own unsealed name and says nothing.
- **Seeds (*proposal*):** beside the four, five small ticks in a neat hand, one crossed out. Wren knows the hand from her mother's lists and doesn't let herself think it.
- **Moment:** Tobin finds Nell's name burning with the rest. He walks out of the hall first.

### 6.8 Floor 9: the root wall (authored)
- **Beat:** the Tithe Roots grown through crypts and foundations; Tobin's father is the first sleeper they can't yet carry out (settled). Hob is the knot of the wall, roots through his coat, mouth moving.
- **Enemies:** root-crawlers, nightmare hounds, the Gleaners hunting in earnest (settled).
- **Gleaners in ch1 stay unexplained:** sheaf-masked figures who try to grab and feed. One leads them, whistling a tune. He never unmasks. Marta hears the tune hummed by Pip back in town and drops a tray (pays off in ch2).
- **Moment:** Tobin tries to cut his father out and the roots bleed red light. Wren: "Not yet. We can't. Not yet." He swings once more anyway, and the stir jumps.

### 6.9 Floor 10: the bell chamber and the Bell-Warden
- **Fight (settled):** the bell digested into a nightmare; its tolling slows time; the heroes fight between the rings.
- **Story meaning (*proposal*):** the bell called the town to the feast every year. In the dream it is the **counter**: each toll is a name, and time slows because being counted is the dread. Between phases it tolls once for every name at the table. In the last phase it swings four more times without a sound, and waits.
- **Retry hook (*settled* paid retry; *proposal* flavour):** the secret idol is one of Amos Gill's carved bell-ringer votives, hidden on a second-half floor with a tell. Gold is the fallback.

### 6.10 After the boss (*settled* beats; *proposal* detail)
- **The bell comes home** with Amos Gill. When it rings over Hollowmere, the whole Vale shakes: Mawrrow's first great stirring in ten years. The roots slacken and **Hob** is carried out.
- **Hob home:** "Where's Nell?" Written for both cases, since Nell is a floor-7 pickup a party may not have found yet:
  - *Nell is home:* she runs to him. He holds her, looks over her head at Tobin, and says nothing in front of the others.
  - *Nell is still below:* Tobin has to say "She's still down there." Hob looks at him for a long time, then walks to the timber yard alone. (The stronger version; the next Undercroft dive is for her.)
  - Either way, that night at the timber yard: "I told you to take her." Tobin asks why his father wanted them out of the square. Hob: "Not mine to tell." (Seeds the conspiracy without naming anyone.)
- **The man who should be dead:** somewhere far up the mountain, in the cold, a man standing in the dark opens his eyes. Unnamed until ch4.
- **The shortcut:** the outdoor tithe gate opens to the halfway bank (settled).
- **The road:** with the mill and the timber yard working, the road to Saltwake opens. Wren checks her wall and finds three heirloom names that weren't anywhere in the Undercroft.
