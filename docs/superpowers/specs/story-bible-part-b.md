# Story bible, Part B: towns 2-6, the main beats, the ending, the voice

**Status: draft for the user (2026-11-03).** Written against `docs/STORY.md` (locked), the dungeon spec (`2026-11-03-hearthdeep-dungeon-design.md`) and Part A (`story-bible-part-a.md`), whose canon pins (Part A section 9) this draft keeps. Every block is tagged *settled* (already in STORY, the spec or Part A's pins, restated) or *proposal* (new here). Anything that bends locked text or Part A is listed in section 6.

Rules carried from Part A: townsfolk talk plain; wishes stay plain; humour under strain; Mawrrow never shouts; no villain explains himself fully; violence between people is rare, short and ugly; no sexual content; no lingering gore. The art never darkens to match the writing.

## 0. Frame decisions this part makes

### 0.1 The sixth town is the Hearth (*proposal*, resolves Part A K1; amendment B1)
STORY 2 counts six towns on one road and says the other five fell after Hollowmere. STORY 6 names five towns plus the Hearth. STORY 7.2 has Mawrrow rise over "the six rebuilt towns" but lists only five contributions. Resolution:

- **The Hearth is a real village**, the sixth stop on the ring road, sitting in the mountain's lap between Stillhollow and Hollowmere. The road closes its ring there, so chapter 6 walks the heroes most of the way home.
- **It is where the Bargain was made.** The Vale's old common harvest festival was held there each year, around a great open hearth at the mouth of the oldest cave, long before any deal. In PY -1 the prayer at that fire was answered. Afterwards the village's people became the **hearth-keepers**: they kept the fire, held the yearly meeting where every town's elder renewed the elder's oath, and kept the tithe roads in repair. Each town still carted its tithe down **its own** mine-mouth (STORY 8.1's ramp under Hollowmere's tower stands); the Hearth's cave is simply the deepest mouth, the demon's throat. So Part A 2.6's one-liner ("every town's tithe road ended there") is reworded: every town's oath ended there.
- **The lair is named after the village.** Mawrrow swallowed the Hearth last, the fifth of the five towns to fall, and rebuilt the village into the antechamber of its own lair. The lair is called the Hearthdeep because the people who first saw its mouth called the village the Hearth.
- **Chapter 6 rebuilds it.** Each Hearthdeep floor returns Hearth residents, and the village rises around its cold hearth: one small ring, no services the player needs, a camp and a story. In STORY 7.2 its contribution is **the last tithe**: the keepers and carters roll the old tithe carts into its mouths full of fire.

### 0.2 Dungeon length (*proposal*; locked-text conflict K3, amendment B2)
STORY 8.4 says the Undercroft is shorter than every later dungeon. STORY 6 says the Hearthdeep is the shortest. Spec decision 29 says later dungeons match the Undercroft's time per half and get richer floors, not more. Proposal: Saltwake, Cinderholt, Thistledown and Stillhollow keep the **ten-floor grammar** (generated floors 2, 4, 6, 7; a first-half set piece on 3; a guardian, a rescue and the bank on 5; a story set piece on 8; an authored floor 9; the boss on 10), and run longer in play time because their floors are richer (more gimmick states, floor events, two set pieces a half). The Hearthdeep has **seven authored floors**, no tool, no generated floors. "Shorter than every later dungeon" then means play time, which is what decision 29 already measures.

### 0.3 The children's table (*proposal*, amendment B3)
Every taken child in the Vale, from every town, sleeps at **one long table at the bottom of the Hearthdeep**, being fed sweets in the dream. "Sleep sweet" in the Gleaners' second bargain (Part A 4.3) means a good dream instead of a bad one; for a child, the good dream is the table, where they are fed and happy and will be eaten last, as the best course. The promise that loved ones "wake first" was true and conditional: first once the debt is paid. It never was. Mawrrow didn't lie. It gives the whole game one line the player feels from chapter 1: **no child comes home**, town after town, until chapter 6.

- Each dungeon's "people left" counter counts only that region's sleepers; it can still reach zero, and nothing is missable (spec 4.4). Wren's wall gains a second column, **"at the table"**, which only chapter 6 can tick.
- **Nell is the exception, and that is the point.** Vane lifted her out of the table and laid her in a coal bin on Undercroft floor 7, beside the empty children's chairs, as the first bait for Tobin (Part A 6.6 stands; the reason is new). Vane reads the tally; he saw the five ticks and knew whose children got away.
- This rewords Part A 2.9 and 6.10: when the Undercroft's counter reaches zero, Wren's list still has about thirty Hollowmere names unticked. Twenty-odd are children; three are heirloom owners (Odile, Jory, Isolde); one is Hester Calder; one is Abner Stroud (whom nobody yet knows is a Gleaner). Wren sees the three heirloom names first and the children second, and the second is what she can't sleep over.

### 0.4 Who the steward used as bait (*proposal*, extends Part A pin 10)
Nell for Tobin (ch1), Odile for Pip (ch2), Jory for Ash (ch3), Isolde for Wren (ch4). Hester (ch5) is not bait: she is Vane's own, kept in the Orrery where he stood beside her for ten years. The pattern is the steward's tell: each chapter's bait is the family of one of the four. By chapter 4 the heroes see it, and Vane knows they see it.

### 0.5 What lieutenants become (*proposal*)
A lieutenant beaten on their authored floor loses the mask. The second bargain breaks, and they wake as a person the party can carry home, unless they refuse. Four outcomes, so no two chapters end the same way:
- **Ivo Sallow** (ch2) comes home to a town that hates him.
- **Dagny Ferrule** (ch3) comes home to be judged.
- **Cantor Lisle** (ch4) unmasks himself, refuses rescue, and walks deeper. He is the Hearthdeep's lieutenant in ch6 (stated reuse).
- **Abner Stroud** (masked ch1, unmasked ch2) is the ch5 lieutenant (stated reuse). He breaks his bargain on his own and dies for it (Part A pin 16).

### 0.6 Cutscenes and the four (*proposal*; reconciling with solo is open question Q1)
Every story scene is written for all four heroes present, in co-op and in solo. Each player's outfit shows (STORY 3). In co-op, the hero a player controls never loses a line to an absent player. Solo's town jobs (STORY 3) stay a gameplay frame: the scenes don't explain where the other three were.

## 1. The towns

### 1.1 Saltwake and the Drowned Arcade (chapter 2, Pip)

**The town (*proposal*).** A fishing harbour on a grey-green estuary: tarred sheds, a stone quay, a weigh-house with a copper roof, smokehouses, and a white lighthouse on the spit. Before the Long Night it was the richest town in the Vale, and the only one with silver coin in ordinary pockets. People here are quick, mean with a joke, and fond of each other in the way of people who have watched boats not come back.

**Its sin (*settled* frame, Part A 2.6; *proposal* detail).** Two ledgers. The true harvest went in one; the figures shown to the deep went in the other. The hidden surplus went downriver on night barges and came back as coin. **Clarification C2:** the harbour clerk **Ivo Sallow** wrote the second ledger; the harbour reeve and elder **Gideon Hask** ordered it, signed it, and grew rich on it. Part A says both "kept" it: Ivo kept the pen, Gideon kept the money.

**The signer elder:** Gideon Hask. He swore the elder's oath at the Hearth in PY 31 and knew the forfeit clause for nineteen years.

**The lieutenant: Ivo Sallow (*proposal*).** He talks, which is why ch2 is where the heroes learn who the Gleaners are (*settled* ch2 truth). Beaten and unmasked on floor 9, he explains the second bargain in a clerk's voice: everyone who serves chose it for someone, and "sweet sleep" is a good dream, which for every child in the Vale is a seat at one table. Then the whistler beside him pulls his own mask off to stop him saying more: **Abner Stroud**, Marta's husband (Part A pin 16). Stroud takes the masked Gleaners and goes. Back in Hollowmere, Tobin is the one who tells Marta (paying off Part A 6.8's dropped tray). His last words to her were a lie about Nell; these are true, and she thanks him for them, which is worse. Ivo, unmasked, sits on the pier and asks Wren if his books balanced. She says no. He lets them carry him.

**The boss's meaning.** The lighthouse brought the boats home, the honest ones and the night barges alike. The dream made it into the thing every fisherman fears: a light that brings you to the teeth. It baits with what you love. Vane laid Odile out in it for that reason.

**The set piece.** Floor 10's dark: nothing visible but the lure, which pulls the camera toward it, and Odile hanging in its light, eyes open and not seeing. Looking away from her is how you survive the fight.

**Pip's arc beat (*settled* owner; *proposal* order).**
1. Gideon, home after floor 5, catches Pip alone reading the ledgers with Wren's notes. He tells her everything and asks her to burn the page "for the town's sake, not mine" (sample scene c, section 4).
2. The boss falls; Odile comes home. That night Pip asks why Gran gave a seven-year-old a real dagger. Odile tells her the truth in four sentences and won't apologise: "I'd do it again. I'd do it to you again."
3. The next evening, while the town gathers to watch Agathe relight the lighthouse, Pip stands on a fish crate and reads Gideon's confession aloud, from memory, word for word.
4. The town beats him in the street. Not to death: Teague and Kip pull them off. His leg never sets right. He is put on the river road the same night. Dorrit stays.
5. Pip walks past the empty plot every time she comes to Saltwake. Nobody ever builds on it (Part A pin 23).

**Loop ties.** The tide is a second clock beside the stir: the party learns to bank on a falling tide. Fishing opens. The ferry shortens the road. Odile's watch service lands in Hollowmere and makes the next dive's stir visible before it starts.

### 1.2 Cinderholt and the Glow Seams (chapter 3, Ash)

**The town (*proposal*).** A mining town cut into red cliffs: terraced houses, ore chutes, a foundry with three chimneys, a bathhouse fed by a hot spring, and the guild hall with its brass doors. Before the Long Night its furnaces never went out, and its iron was the best in the Vale. People are blunt, proud of their hands, and suspicious of anyone whose hands are soft.

**Its sin (*settled*, STORY 6; *proposal* detail).** The furnaces had no fuel; the forests on the red cliffs were cut out a generation before the Bargain. In PY 38 the guild found warm rock deep in the seams and followed it. By PY 45 they had dug a breach into Mawrrow's own heat and were piping its breath into the furnaces: free fire, endless iron. Every year the demon digested a little less and needed a little more. In PY 50 that was the difference: **the last tithe fell short for everyone because Cinderholt was stealing the stove** (*settled* ch3 truth).

**The signer elder: Osric Varn**, guild master. He ordered the dig. When the heat-sickness started killing diggers in PY 46, he blamed the shift-boss's crew for going too deep, which is the origin of Dagny Ferrule's fury (Part A 4.4).

**The story gate (*proposal*; spec 4.4 still holds).** The Old Seam is mandatory and scripted. The counter always shows Jory, so nothing is missed. The party can clear the dungeon, but the chapter doesn't close and the road to Thistledown doesn't open until Jory is home. The only way in is the word Ash buys (below). On the next dive the ward opens to that word, and Jory comes home as floor 8's own rescue.

**The lieutenant: Dagny Ferrule.** She doesn't try to feed the four; she argues with them while her crew does it. "You lot think we dug down there for fun? Your town ate off our iron for five years and called us animals." Beaten, unmasked, she asks one thing: "Is he still on his engine?" She comes home, walks into the guild hall and tells Cinderholt that Osric Varn signed the dig orders. Then she hands herself to the guild for judgement. Their sentence, in the epilogue, is the seam-capping.

**The boss's meaning.** The engine carried the stolen fire's ore out of the deep. In the dream it runs on the breath, and it can't stop because nobody ever told it the shift was over. Ebben is asleep at the controls, smiling.

**The set piece: the trade.** It isn't in the dungeon. After the first visit to floor 8, Ash climbs to the warm fissure in the high scree at night (Part A pin 22). The voice is polite. It knows where Jory is, and it will tell him the word that opens the seam. It asks a small price: "Give me one name you've already taken from me." Ash says **Wynn Cobb**. Down in Hollowmere, the lamps on the lamp lane go out one by one, and Wynn's house goes dark (Part A A11: the only re-take in the game). The player sees the lamps go out from the scree, with Ash.

**Ash's arc beat.**
1. Jory comes home. He has been asleep and doesn't know the price. He laughs, picks Ash up off the ground, and asks why he's crying.
2. Tuppence won't go near Ash for a week.
3. Pip knows where Ash goes at night. When Wynn's house goes dark she understands and says nothing for two days. Then Wren sees Pip's face and asks.
4. Wren keeps it (Part A 3.5). Tobin finds out from Marta, who heard Wynn's neighbours. He breaks Ash's nose in the timber yard: one punch, no fight. That night he sets the nose himself and tells Ash he's still on the team because "I'm not losing anyone else, not even you" (Part A pin 22).
5. **The midpoint turn (*proposal*).** On the next dive, as the Foreman's Engine falls, a voice speaks from the dark beyond the arena: a man's voice, dry and courteous. "You have your mother's way of standing, boy. Tell Hester I asked." Nobody is there. It is the steward, unnamed (Part A pin 12). From here on the heroes know there is a person behind the Gleaners, and that he knows their families.

**Loop ties.** Heat raises stir faster, so the party banks earlier. The forge's best upgrades open. The ore line hauls materials. Wynn's collapse seconds are gone until ch6, and the player feels it on every timer.

### 1.3 Thistledown and the Hive Choir (chapter 4, Wren)

**The town (*proposal*).** An orchard village on the high meadows around a honey-coloured abbey: hives in white rows, cider presses, a cloister garden, and a bell-less chapel where the choir sang instead. Before the Long Night it was the Vale's gentlest town and its most certain one. People are soft-spoken and kind, and very sure they are good.

**Its sin (*settled* frame, Part A 2.6; *proposal* detail).** The abbey preached the Tithe Feast as a sacrament: "Who eats at the table is held in the hand." Nobody in Thistledown questioned sitting down, because their abbess told them it was holy. Their tithe was honey and wax, weighed heavy and fed nothing; the deep can't eat sweetness. Fifty years of padding. And the abbey wrote the pageant that every town's schoolmaster taught (Bram Tolley's in Hollowmere, Part A 5.2).

**The signer elder: Abbess Morwenna Pyle** (70s). She swore the elder's oath at the Hearth, went home, and wrote a sermon. She believes now, and believed then, that a sacred lie kept six towns fed for fifty years and that this was worth it.

**The lieutenant: Cantor Lisle.** He doesn't argue. He invites. "You've been hungry for ten years. Sit down. It's not a trick; it's the only true thing anyone ever told this Vale." Beaten, he takes off his own mask, and he has a kind face. He thanks them. Then he walks into the dark below the choir loft, singing, and the Gleaners part for him. He is the only servant who asked Mawrrow for nothing.

**The boss's meaning.** The choir sang the feast blessing every year. The queen is fed by everyone and feeds no one. The abbey built a faith around feeding something, and the dream made it a hive.

**The set piece: Vane in the orchard (*proposal*).** After the boss, with the bees coming home and blossom on the trees, a man in an old elder's coat walks up the orchard path in full daylight. He is forty-four to look at, grey at the temples, courteous. The Gleaners don't come with him. He introduces himself: **Corwin Vane**, elder of Hollowmere (*settled* ch4 truth: alive, and made the deal). ### 1.4 Stillhollow and the Orrery (chapter 5, all four)

They laid in mules, furs and food at the head of the pass, ready to leave over the mountain in the summer of PY 50. ### 1.5 The Hearth and the Hearthdeep (chapter 6, the finale)

**Its sin (*proposal*).** The **keepers' share.** By old arrangement every town paid the Hearth a wage for keeping the fire and the oath: a measure off the top of every tithe, before it went down. Their defence, which is true and incomplete: their own small tithe was always paid on time. ## 3. The one true ending

STORY 7's four stages are *settled*. Everything below is *proposal*, and every loss is unconditional: STORY 7 says the ending is the same whatever the rebuild level, so a fuller Vale makes stage 2 easier, never saves anyone.

### 3.1 Stage 1: the waking
Tobin lays Col Stroud on the cart at the top of the hall, and the hall goes dark. Mawrrow wakes with nothing in it but sixty years of hunger. It doesn't roar. It says, from everywhere, in the voice of a host who has found the larder empty: "Oh. Oh, you've cleared the table." The Hearthdeep starts collapsing upward, and the four ride it to the surface (*settled*) on the Hearth's own carts, with the children in the wagons behind them and Vane chained to the tailboard. They come out of the throat into daylight above the Hearth's rebuilt houses.

### 3.2 Stage 2: the fight in the open
Mawrrow rises out of the mountain into a clear blue sky above the six rebuilt towns (*settled*). It is beautiful in the way of a storm: a body of ember and dark harvest gold, mouths where a face should be. The residents fight beside the heroes (*settled*), each town's thing in turn:

- **Hollowmere:** Amos Gill rings the bell, and every toll slows the demon the way the Bell-Warden slowed the heroes.
- **Saltwake:** Agathe Voss turns the lighthouse beam inland and holds it on the demon's eyes.
- **Cinderholt:** Hilde Saxby and Ebben Ferrule fire the two bore-cannons from the cliff terraces. Dagny loads.
- **Thistledown:** Sister Clemency sends the abbey's swarm into its mouths.
- **Stillhollow:** Edda Quill and the Provost focus the lens. Wyck holds it on the demon long after Edda tells him to stop. He is blind from then on.
- **The Hearth:** Gerda Brand lights the great hearth with honest wood, and the carters roll the old tithe carts off the cave-mouth ledge into its mouths as it rises, loaded with fire. The last tithe.

**Odile's death.** Mawrrow's hand comes down on Hollowmere's square, where the long tables stood. Children are being carried up from the Hearth road into the square, and the feast gate is the only way out of it. Odile takes the gate, the same post where she counted heads on the Long Night, and holds it with a watch pike while the children are got through. The hand closes on the gate. When it lifts she is under the arch, and she is dead. Pip sees it from the bell tower. The game does not show the body; it shows Pip, and Odile's chair by her door afterwards. Pip takes the watch service from here on (section 1.1).

### 3.3 Stage 3: inside the maw
The demon swallows the four (*settled*). Inside is a feast hall again, the first one: a long table, and at its head Mawrrow's heart, where the original contract is held (*settled*): a great ember heart with the Bargain written round it in the script, and Vane's own small human heart sealed inside it as surety. The heroes fight the heart (*settled*): a playable boss in which it serves the table, sending a course of nightmares from every dungeon, while the written contract shields it.

Cantor Lisle is there, seated at the table, eyes closed, at peace. He came down to be eaten and he was. Nobody touches him.

Between the fight's phases Mawrrow makes each hero one last offer, politely. Tobin: his mother back, Jessamy, alive, in exchange for one hero's seat. Pip: Odile back, now, today. Wren: her mother as she was, before the vote, before any of it. Ash: everything, if he lets Wren's word fail, and the voice from the fissure is gentle about it. All four refuse; Ash refuses last (Part A 3.5).

**The finish: each heirloom strikes once (*settled*), and the legendaries pay off:**
- **Marshfell** (Tobin) splits the table, so there is no table to be seated at.
- **Last Watch** (Pip) takes the blow that Mawrrow aims at Wren, and the shield breaks doing it. It is the only legendary that comes out of the finale damaged: Pip hangs it in Odile's house, and the epilogue's smith work mends it.
- **Brother's Due** (Ash) cuts Vane's heart free of the contract.
- **True North** (Wren) burns the warm colour of the lantern from the prologue (Part A 2.11), and with it she writes.

**Speaking against it.** Wren stands before Mawrrow, awake and unbound (*settled*), and reads the forfeit clause aloud in the script. Then she writes under it, in the demon's own letters, with her own unbound hand: **the table is empty.** The demon is owed nothing, and a thing that can only eat what is owed has nothing left to eat.

**What it costs Wren.** The script is the demon's handwriting. When the demon ends, so does its alphabet. Her magic goes out mid-letter. Every ward in the Vale fails, every charm stops. Tuppence's collar becomes a collar. Wren knew. Isolde told her in ch4, and she didn't tell the others (her last secret, and the only one she's proud of).

### 3.4 Stage 4: the end of the Bargain
**Mawrrow's end.** It doesn't die like a monster. It dies like a fire with no wood: smaller and politer, until it's the size of a dog at the hearth, then a warm red stone on a bare cave floor. Its last words are, "Thank you. It was a lovely meal." By morning the stone is cold, and Ash, who listened to it for three years, picks it up and throws it in the lake.

**Vane's end.** Outside, on the high scree at dawn, Ash gives Corwin Vane his heart back. Vane holds it and it goes quiet in his hands, and he gets to be a hundred and four. He asks Ash to tell Hester nothing kind about him. He dies in the sun inside a minute. Ash buries him on the scree beside the fissure, which is cold now, under no stone. No churchyard in the Vale will take him. Bram Tolley breaks the old memorial stone in Hollowmere's churchyard with a hammer, which is the bravest thing he has ever done. Tobin doesn't go to the burial and never asks about it. Hester never visits the grave. Ash goes once a year.

**The fields.** The fields go back to being ordinary fields (*settled*). The Vale has to farm honestly now.

### 3.6 Epilogue: the honest harvest (*settled* frame; *proposal* detail)
Autumn, PY 61. The first harvest grown without a deal is thin: blighted rows, short sheaves, a hungry winter coming, the kind the Vale had before the Bargain. It isn't enough, and it is shared. Long tables in the square, lanterns lit by hand. No names written anywhere. Before anyone eats, Amos Gill rings the bell, and the children of the Vale, awake, are the loudest people at the table.

The last scene is the nightly meeting in Wren's house: all four, the cat on the table, the wall behind them. Pip asks what they do now. Tobin says, "Eat. Then plant." Wren says nothing. Ash says it'll be a hard winter. Tobin says, "We've had worse," and for once nobody makes a joke.

**TOBIN:** You're late.
**PIP:** I was helping.
**ASH:** Helping who?
**PIP:** Saltwake. With its civic duties.
**TOBIN:** They broke his leg, Pip.
**PIP:** I didn't break anything. I read. Out loud. Wren taught me; blame her.
**WREN:** Sit down.
**PIP:** Every word of it was true.
**ASH:** That's never been your problem. *(Beat.)* You do it beautifully, though. Like a bell.
**TOBIN:** Don't.
**ASH:** I'm not encouraging her. I'm describing her.
*(Pip sits. Tuppence gets up, walks the length of the table and lies down on her hands.)*
**PIP:** Even the cat's on your side.
**WREN:** The cat's on the side of whoever's warm.
**TOBIN:** Dorrit gave me a fish today. For the house. Put it in my hands, said "for the big one", and looked straight through you.
**PIP:** Good fish?
**TOBIN:** Lovely fish.
**PIP:** Rot it. *(Pause.)* Gran's home.
**WREN:** I know.
**PIP:** She told me why she packed the dagger. She said she'd do it again. She said she'd do it *to me* again. *(She laughs. It doesn't work.)* So that's nice. It's nice to be wanted.
*(Nobody speaks. Ash slides his cup across to her.)*
**ASH:** It's Kip's. It's terrible.
**PIP:** Everything Kip makes is terrible.
**TOBIN:** He'll be on the river road by now. Gideon. Somebody should've…
**WREN:** Should've what?
**TOBIN:** I don't know. Walked him to the edge of town. So he didn't go alone.
**PIP:** He sold the grain that would've—
**WREN:** We all know what he sold.
*(Long pause. Wren gets up, takes her brush, and goes to the wall. She doesn't paint anything. She stands there with the brush until Tobin takes it out of her hand.)*
**TOBIN:** Same time tomorrow.
**PIP:** Where else would we go?

### 4b. Wren and her mother's script (chapter 4)
*Isolde's new room in Wren's house. Night. A slate on the desk. Isolde writes one letter of the script with a fingertip; it glows and goes out.*

**ISOLDE:** Again. Your downstroke's too slow. It isn't a picture. It's a word; it wants to be said.
**WREN:** You taught yourself this.
**ISOLDE:** From fifty years of tally pages. Mine, mostly.
*(Wren writes. A faint light, gone.)*
**WREN:** Like that?
**ISOLDE:** Like that. You've been reading it since you were twelve. From a cat's collar.
**WREN:** From the collar and the trapdoor. You left it everywhere.
**ISOLDE:** I left it where it was needed.
**WREN:** The page in the scriptorium. The five ticks. Odile, Hob, Jory. And one crossed out.
**ISOLDE:** Nell.
**WREN:** You could have taken ten.
**ISOLDE:** I could have taken none. Ten children gone from the table is a search before the meal. Five is mischief. I did the figures, Wren. It's what I do.
**WREN:** Tilly Rowe sat next to me at school. Her name's on my wall.
**ISOLDE:** I know whose names are on your wall.
**WREN:** You could read the contract. All of it. What it says about someone unbound, standing at the table.
**ISOLDE:** I could read it.
**WREN:** Did you save me because I'm yours? Or because you needed someone who could stand there?
*(A long silence.)*
**ISOLDE:** Both answers are true. Pick the one you can live with.
**WREN:** That isn't—
**ISOLDE:** It's the only honest thing I have, and I'm giving it to you instead of a kind one.
**WREN:** If it ends, does this end? *(The slate.)*
**ISOLDE:** Every letter. Every ward. The cat's collar. You.
**WREN:** My lantern stayed lit. Everyone else's went out.
**ISOLDE:** I filled the oil. That's all I did.
**WREN:** Is that true?
**ISOLDE:** Again. Your downstroke.
*(Wren writes. The letter flares and goes out.)*

### 4c. An elder confesses (chapter 2)
*Saltwake, the weigh-house steps at dusk. Pip has the true ledger open on her knees. Bunting going up across the square for the lighthouse. Gideon Hask comes down the quay slowly and sits two steps above her.*

**GIDEON:** You read figures now. Who taught you?
**PIP:** Wren. She's better at it. She's asleep.
**GIDEON:** That column's mine. The neat one. Ivo wrote it, but it's mine. I told him what to write, and I signed under it every autumn for nineteen years.
**PIP:** Why are you telling me?
**GIDEON:** Because you'll have it worked out by Thursday, and I'd sooner you heard it from someone who was there. We had a hungry year in thirty-one. Your gran will remember. The deep wanted a quarter. A quarter of nothing is still nothing. So I kept two books, and the barges went downriver, and the coin came back, and nobody in this town buried a child that winter. Not one.
**PIP:** And the year after?
**GIDEON:** The year after, I kept two books because it had worked. And the year after that because I'd a new roof. *(Pause.)* I knew the clause. I swore to it at the Hearth. I thought, they're all cheating, every town. One more won't tip it.
**PIP:** It tipped.
**GIDEON:** It tipped.
**PIP:** What do you want?
**GIDEON:** Burn the page. Not for me. Look at them. Ten years asleep and they've woken to nothing. They need one thing to be true, and what they want is that it wasn't us. Let them have it. Let them have a reeve.
**PIP:** And you get your house on the quay.
**GIDEON:** I get to watch the boats come in. That's all I ever wanted, girl. Every one of them, home.
*(Pip closes the ledger.)*
**PIP:** I'll think about it.
**GIDEON:** That's all I ask.
*(He goes up the quay. Pip sits a long time. Then she opens the ledger again and reads it under her breath, from the top, until she has it by heart.)*

### 4d. Vane and Ash (chapter 6)
*The road down to the Hearth, a night camp. Vane chained to a cart wheel. The others asleep. Ash feeds the fire.*

**ASH:** You're not asleep.
**VANE:** I haven't slept in sixty years. Ask it. You've been working up to it for three miles.
**ASH:** Did you love them?
**VANE:** Whom?
**ASH:** Any of them. Your wife. Your boy. Bettany. Us.
**VANE:** My wife was Ellen. I dug her grave myself in ground so hard it took two days. Bettany was eleven and I could count her ribs through her dress. I signed for her. You I've met twice.
**ASH:** That's not an answer.
**VANE:** It's the one I have.
**ASH:** You read the clause.
**VANE:** Every word.
**ASH:** And you thought what? That we'd keep up?
**VANE:** I thought, it's sixty harvests off. I'll be dead. *(Beat.)* I was right about one of those.
*(Ash laughs before he can stop himself, and hates it.)*
**ASH:** It talked to me. Up on the scree. Three years.
**VANE:** I know. It talked to me for sixty. Did it ever lie to you?
**ASH:** No.
**VANE:** No. It never lies. That's the trouble. It tells you the true thing and lets you do the sum.
**ASH:** It told me where Jory was.
**VANE:** And the price?
**ASH:** A man called Wynn.
**VANE:** *(Quietly.)* Then you know everything about me there is to know. You've only paid once.
**ASH:** Don't.
**VANE:** I'm not comforting you; I've forgotten how. I'm telling you what it does. It finds the one you'd pay anything for, and then it asks for something small.
**ASH:** When it's over, you get your heart back.
**VANE:** I do.
**ASH:** And you want to die.
**VANE:** I want to sleep. In the ground, if that's the only bed left.
**ASH:** Tobin says we should leave you down there.
**VANE:** Tobin is right. *(Pause.)* Will you?
*(Ash puts another stick on the fire, and doesn't answer.)*
